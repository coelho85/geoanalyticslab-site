Pillar 01 · ESG spatial risk assessment & compliance

We analyse your locations using the best available spatial data.

Regulations such as the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) and the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) ask companies to know where their products come from and what is happening on that land. We turn location data into risk evidence your sustainability, sourcing and compliance teams can act on.

Background

Why location matters for ESG risk.

ESG began as an investment term for the environmental, social and governance factors that can materially affect an asset or a business. It now covers wider sustainability concerns and is used across the private sector to judge how a company affects people and the environment.

Many of these risks show up locally, and they vary a great deal depending on where a site, farm or supplier is located. Cutting freshwater use matters most where baseline water stress is already high. Commodity sourcing is most exposed to deforestation risk where forest frontiers are still advancing. This is the idea behind ESG mapping: model the geographic context around an operation as spatial layers of environmental, social, economic, regulatory and political data, then overlay real or planned locations to see which ESG factors must be prioritized for action.1

ESG mapping does not measure a company's performance. It looks upstream, at the pre-existing conditions a business has to take into account. Risk runs both ways: inbound risk from the local context to the operation, such as water scarcity, floods or land conflicts, and outbound risk from the operation to communities and ecosystems.1,2

So, if you are looking to use geospatial technology to uncover the underlying risks in your supply chain, get in touch.

What we offer

Services in this pillar.

Tailored sourcing risk assessments that combine your site and supplier locations with the best available spatial data, informing your sustainability reporting and supporting compliance with EUDR, CSRD and other regulations.

01.1

Accurate locations

We review your location points, reports, addresses and any other location information, and convert them into precise coordinates you can rely on.

01.2

Methodology review

Independent review of existing risk assessment protocols: indicator choice, data quality, weighting and aggregation, with clear recommendations to improve how risk is detected and reported.

01.3

Supply chain risk assessment

We use all available information to calculate the physical (water, climate, biodiversity), social and regulatory risks across your supply chain.

01.4

EUDR due diligence

Risk assessment and geolocation evidence for the commodities covered by the EU Deforestation Regulation, including satellite-based verification of deforestation-free production.

01.5

CSRD / ESRS spatial evidence

Location-based data for the environmental topics of the ESRS, such as climate, water and biodiversity, to support materiality assessment and disclosure.

01.6

Implementation roadmaps

Step-by-step plans to move spatial risk models from analysis into everyday sourcing, compliance and investment workflows.

Experience

Built on years of applied risk work.

Our team has delivered spatial risk work for companies, financial institutions and sustainability standards. That includes:

  • technical leadership of a global water risk platform used by companies and investors, keeping more than 80 global spatial layers up to date
  • advising corporate clients, financial institutions and policy makers on water scarcity and financial risk exposure
  • reviewing sourcing risk methods for agricultural supply chains across several regions
  • integrating high-resolution ESG datasets into risk models for international sourcing standards
  • coordinating EUDR verification for large agricultural importers
